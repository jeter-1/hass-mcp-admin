# HAMCP-135 exact governed inverse

This local increment permits a new elevated owner approval for an exact inverse
of a durably verified prefix of the complete five-object HAMCP-135 transfer.
It does not close D2 or authorize household application. Restoring the script
returns its original unconditional close and internal retry, including the known
lack of fresh caller revalidation. It neither repairs that weakness nor reverses
physical commands.

## Authority and compatibility

Only the internal F3 rollback path issues `hamcp135-exact-inverse-v1`. It requires
the unchanged shipped `caller_owned_retry` forward proof, exact source plan/hash,
terminal source task, immutable sequence/declarations, and contiguous verified
children with durable dispatch intent, one dispatch, exact readback fingerprints
and consumed-approval witnesses. A status string, readable body or verified no-op
is insufficient. A separately approved minimal-removal plan gains no inverse
script authority. No public flag, caller context or copied marker grants it.

A distinct inverse proof is carried in existing operation risk evidence and bound
through existing policy, review, plan and preparation hashes. It binds all five
source members, receipt hashes, selected reverse indices and the complete observed
state. Structural proof is recomputed on load; durable source evidence is checked
for approval/apply and each whole-bundle preflight. Projection and post-intent
readback retain their existing limited authority if source evidence becomes
unavailable; no dependent new dispatch is allowed. Source evidence refusal is
local. Genuine new durable write failures retain normal fail-closed protections.

There is one deterministic inverse attempt per exact source plan/hash/task. Calling
the existing rollback interface again returns that attempt; it does not refresh
approval, expiry or execution ownership. A cached beta.16 prohibited inverse stays
unchanged and prohibited. The internal path creates a distinct eligible attempt
and updates the source's existing rollback link. A short existing projection
transaction serializes plan/link issuance. A crash leaving the link before the
new plan can be retried before any approval exists. No plan-store scan, persisted
schema/version change, normalizer change or historical proof upgrade is involved.

The beta.16 fixture is produced by the exact shipped writer, from committed
synthetic bodies before hashing. Its original serialized records and generation
provenance are under `tests/fixtures/hamcp135_rollback/`. Household bodies are not
committed. Historical forward hashes and the forward proof implementation remain
unchanged.

Cleaner owns both call sites in one configuration operation. Each inverse payload
is the immutable captured prior body, with existing provider normalization and
validation unchanged. The complete five-object state is checked before the first
write, before every later child and at final verification: restored members at
original hashes, remaining selected members at forward hashes, untouched members
at original hashes. Unexpected external restoration is drift, not our receipt.

If the observed bundle is incomplete or drifted, the script is withheld. Matching
verified callers may receive an explicitly partial plan with fresh approval;
excluded hashes are bound and continue to be checked. The partial plan cannot
later acquire script restoration by rerequesting rollback. Existing result fields
list selected/excluded operation IDs; plan/task operation records report actual
restored and unrestored outcomes. Task success means the selected subset was
verified; `rollback_partial_plan_created` never means full five-object recovery.

All five resource locks are included even for F1. Verified intermediate inverse
children retain the entire immutable lock union. The next exact claimed child
receives it through one atomic compare-and-transfer, with new fencing generations,
before its full preflight. The transfer checks the complete observed owner set,
keys, scopes, modes, evidence, leases and fences against the immutable declarations
and durable verified predecessor. Other F3 sequences retain their existing rules.

Retention is derived from persisted plan, task, declarations and child receipts;
an in-memory callback is not its authority. A restart with live predecessor leases
can continue the same sequence. Expired leases, partial sets and conflict holds do
not grant fresh admission. An expired predecessor refuses the successor and ends
the selected inverse as partial; it cannot silently acquire a new set. A crash
after transfer but before token persistence stops the interrupted pre-intent child
after its claim expires. The existing exact unrecorded-set reconciliation binds
the tokens before releasing them. Neither case repeats a provider dispatch.

Terminal settlement releases only exact proven regular ownership; unknown outcomes
retain the complete union as conflict holds. Retained terminal ownership remains
visible in existing reconciliation diagnostics. A missing or contradictory receipt
cannot authorize cleanup. Repeated requests keep the original bound attempt;
partial termination does not refresh approval or promise that the inverse can be
resumed. Further recovery must use an explicitly reviewed supported procedure.

## Approval, uncertainty and recovery

The new exact inverse requires a fresh authenticated elevated plan approval bound
to its current hash and visible consequence. Historical forward approval is only
source evidence. Current Core/provider authority and existing dispatch-generation
checks apply. The shipped configuration contract has no independent installation
field to invent; existing current Core identity/authority remains mandatory in
the assembled Engineering runtime. No old approval or stale authority lease is
reused. Deterministic preflight refusal does not consume approval.

After durable intent, repeated apply/recovery can only join or reconcile that
attempt. A lost reply does not authorize another write. Cancellation after
possible dispatch remains refused. Unknown source dispatch, corrupt/missing
receipts, stale state, held ownership, failed validation or unavailable current
authority block dependent writes. Existing reconciliation may establish a verified
outcome; generating an inverse never clears holds or rewrites terminal history.

## D2 and acceptance plan

Configuration restoration, runtime activation and physical behavior are separate
claims. Saved baseline bytes do not prove reload completion, that old runs stopped,
or that a garage command was undone. Locks do not exclude external HA edits or
household runs. Reads/writes are non-atomic and cannot provide a physical interlock.

The focused disposable Core 2026.9.4 campaign uses the cached immutable image,
exact synthetic bodies and fake cover/report services, without network or
production mounts. It assembles actual script/automation components, reloads every
forward/inverse intermediate, exercises old/new caller-script transitions, and
observes saving bytes versus reloading an active run. It also demonstrates the
original retry after a caller condition changes. This is local evidence only;
reuse the reviewed forward campaign separately, without claiming a new full run.

Before a later authorized household application, independent review and release
integration must complete, and the installed artifact must be verified. The owner
must accept the retry consequence and a supported activation/run-settlement
procedure. At every step reconcile the entire five-object state and durable task
receipts; stop on uncertainty or drift. Prepare and approve a fresh inverse through
the supported route only. Do not force a household failure, operate the garage or
infer settlement from saved bytes. D2 stays open until that procedure and recovery
are accepted under separate live authorization.
